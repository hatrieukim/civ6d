# Strategy (example)

Copy this file to `strategy.md` and make it yours. Claude follows `strategy.md` for every decision. This example sums up common Deity-level advice from CivFanatics, Steam Community guides and well-known players (PotatoMcWhiskey, Ursa Ryan, TheSaxyGamer).

---

The "standard" way to play Civ VI fits in one idea: **the game rewards compounding**. Every point of Production, Science or Gold you get early turns into more points later. Good players are not the ones who fight best; they are the ones who **start compounding earliest**. Seven principles follow from that.

**1. Expand without stopping.** Civ VI does not punish wide empires the way Civ V did. Each new city is a permanent new source of yields, so a Settler is always the best thing to build while there is free land. The target good players aim for: 10–12 cities before turn 100 (Standard speed). The *Colonization* card (+50% Settler production) and the *Monumentality* Golden Age dedication (buy Settlers with Faith) are the main tools. Everything else — wonders, religion, a big army — comes after.

**2. The first 50 turns decide the game.** Standard opening: Scout → Slinger → Settler → Settler, a Pantheon in the capital as early as possible (God King or a cheap Holy Site), and **chop forests / harvest resources** with Builders to rush Settlers. Governor Magnus (no population loss when building Settlers, +50% chop yields) in the capital is the core trick. Pingala goes to the main science/culture city.

**3. The first district is a Commercial Hub or Harbor**, not a Campus, in every city except the capital. Why: each one gives an extra trade route, domestic trade routes give Food + Production so new cities grow fast, and roads build themselves. Campus, Theater Square and Industrial Zone come once the economy is running.

**4. The army is insurance, not the goal.** Keep enough units that the AI does not dare declare war (the AI looks at military strength when deciding). If you fight, fight early: taking a neighbour with Archers/Swordsmen before turn 80 is cheaper than any other way to expand, and other civs forget quickly. After the Medieval era, war almost always costs more than building.

**5. Pick a victory before turn 100, then go all in.** Fast winners never "do a bit of everything". Science: Campus + Industrial Zone everywhere, Great Engineers at the end. Culture: Theater Squares, wonders, National Parks, Rock Bands. Domination: snowball, one push per era as soon as new units arrive. Religious and Diplomatic victories are considered the weakest.

**6. Never leave resources idle.** Empty trade route slots, hoarded Gold, unspent Faith, a Great Person about to be taken by someone else — all of that is wasted compounding. Good players spend nearly everything every 10 turns.

**7. Era Score and Golden Ages.** Watch the Era Score bar; do "firsts" (meet city-states, kill barbarians, build districts) to stay in Golden Ages, especially in the Classical era to get Monumentality.

In one sentence: **more cities, earlier districts, spend everything, pick one victory and stick to it.** The rest is detail.
